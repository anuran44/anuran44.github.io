The Challenge of Synthetic Media

Deepfakes pose a severe threat to social trust. Standard deepfake detectors act as opaque black boxes, providing users with a "fake/real" label but no rationale. My research approaches this as a Social Computing challenge: building a computer vision tool that human moderators and everyday users can actually understand and trust.

Method & Public Application

Developed an open-source Python framework that surfaces mathematical anomalies (FFT, Error Level Analysis, PRNU) directly to the user via a clean UX interface. As a public-benefit application, it translates complex generative AI artifacts into verifiable, physics-based visual evidence, ensuring users aren't forced to blindly trust an AI's classification.

AI Fact Verification: Combating Textual Misinformation

RQ: How can we design adaptive, retrieval-augmented NLP systems that support human fact-checkers in social computing environments without cognitive overload?
Complementing Visual Forensics with NLP

While deepfakes manipulate the visual realm, algorithmic text generation degrades factual ecosystems. Static RAG pipelines often overwhelm users with redundant documents or obscure their reasoning. I address this critical human-AI interaction bottleneck by optimizing how language models retrieve and present factual evidence to human readers.

Method & UX Design

I developed a framework combining Gemma 3 LLM reasoning with an adaptive semantic retrieval algorithm. Crucially, I designed the system's workflow to prioritize human readability—mathematically terminating iterative retrieval once semantic similarity stabilizes. This presents users with concise, verifiable claim-evidence pairs, improving FEVER benchmark performance while significantly reducing the cognitive load for human moderators.

TxtVis: Visualizing Multimodal Latent Spaces

RQ: How do we build visual, interactive front-ends that help non-expert users intuitively interpret complex multimodal embeddings?
Problem & Motivation

The optimization dynamics of multimodal representations (text and image) are mathematically dense. Without human-friendly visualization tools, evaluating model bias and geometric separation remains a black box for researchers and the public alike.

Method & Front-End Engineering

I built a PyTorch backend utilizing Skip-Gram embeddings and Siamese CNNs, explicitly coupling it with a highly interactive Streamlit UX front-end. This UI allows users to dynamically monitor PCA projections and visually track embedding evolution in real-time during training, prioritizing cognitive clarity for complex metric learning tasks.
